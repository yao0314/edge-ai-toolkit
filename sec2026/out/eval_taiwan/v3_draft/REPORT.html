EuroSAT MobileNetV2: deep structured pruning, teacher-guided recovery, static INT8

Team: Valiant Joy Model: Valiant Joy Submission: valiant_joy_Model.onnx (input (1, 3, 224, 224), ONNX opset 13, static QDQ, 0.60 MB)

Pipeline

  1. Start: the provided student_mobilenetv2_distilled.pt (MobileNetV2, 2.24 M parameters, FP32).
  2. Structured pruning, 60% of channels. Channels are removed, not masked: the exported graph keeps MobileNetV2's topology (52 convolutions, 17 depthwise, 10 residual adds) with fewer channels, so parameters, file size and MACs all really shrink.
  3. Recovery: BatchNorm re-estimation, then 8 epochs of fine-tuning distilled from the provided ResNet-50 teacher (KD temperature 4, alpha 0.7).
  4. Static INT8: QDQ, per-channel weights, percentile calibration on training images; exported with the TorchScript exporter at opset 13 and the input pinned to (1, 3, 224, 224).

What we tried, kept and discarded

- Measure the weakest class, not only the mean. Sweeping the pruning ratio from 0% to 60%, every point up to 20% sits inside the +-0.36-point measurement band of the mean; the worst-class recall is the statistic that moves, and it located the cliff between 50% and 60%. - Spend on recovery, not the cut. At 60%, teacher-guided recovery lifted the weakest class by 7.8 points over hard-label recovery, for a few lines of configuration; at 50% it made no difference. The collapse at 60% is a recovery limit rather than a capacity limit, which is what let us ship the deeper point. - Profile on the target. The same two ONNX files read 3.13x on our laptop and 8.94x on the edge CPU: a large out-of-order core with a deep cache hides most of what compression buys, so choosing a ratio from laptop latency alone would read the gradient about three times too shallow. - Discarded: per-layer ratio allocation. Better at matched size, but it stalls at 46.6% overall, because the cheap parameters are a bounded pool. - The cost we report: the weakest class falls to 92.6%, below the baseline's 94.7% (on the second-machine check it is PermanentCrop, the class that is also weakest for the baseline). One class carries most of the loss; we report it rather than average it away.
